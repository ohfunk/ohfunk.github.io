<section class="page-hero"><div class="container"><p class="eyebrow">Join the lab</p><h1>Start small. Ask big questions.</h1><p>We are especially excited to meet undergraduate researchers and rotation students who want to explore development, genomics, and computation.</p></div></section>
<article class="container prose"><h2>Projects you could help shape</h2><div class="opportunity"><span class="tag">WET LAB</span><div><h3>Cell fusion as a genetic switch</h3><p>Use <em>C. elegans</em> genetics, microscopy, and careful staging to learn how fusion changes developmental programs. A great entry point for trainees who want hands-on experience with worms and developmental biology.</p></div></div><div class="opportunity"><span class="tag">GENOMICS</span><div><h3>Reading individual nuclei</h3><p>Help optimize and analyze single-nucleus RNA-seq and ATAC-seq experiments that reveal how nuclei differ within a shared cytoplasm.</p></div></div><div class="opportunity"><span class="tag">COMPUTE</span><div><h3>Making sense of multi-omic data</h3><p>Build reproducible workflows in R or Python, visualize developmental trajectories, and develop models that turn large datasets into testable hypotheses.</p></div></div><h2>What we value</h2><p>You do not need prior worm, coding, or genomics experience. Curiosity, consistency, and an interest in learning are the important starting points. We will match a project’s scope to your experience and available time, then make space for you to own a meaningful piece of it.</p><div class="contact-card"><h3>Interested?</h3><p>Send Owen a short note at <a href="mailto:ofunk@uwyo.edu">ofunk@uwyo.edu</a> with your background, what interests you, and what you hope to learn.</p></div></article>
