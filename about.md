<section class="page-hero"><div class="container"><p class="eyebrow">About the lab</p><h1>Science with a sense of wonder.</h1><p>The Funk Lab is an emerging research group at the University of Wyoming studying the regulatory logic of syncytial gene expression.</p></div></section>
<article class="container prose"><h2>Owen H. Funk, PhD</h2><p>Owen is a computational biologist and postdoctoral fellow whose work combines experimental genetics, genomics, and quantitative analysis to understand how cells establish identity during development. He is building an independent program focused on cell fusion and gene regulation in <em>C. elegans</em>.</p><p>His research spans RNA-seq, ATAC-seq, single-cell and single-nucleus multi-omics, imaging, and developmental biology. Before joining the University of Wyoming, Owen earned a PhD in Human Genetics at the University of Michigan, where he studied how the histone variant H3.3 establishes neuronal chromatin and identity in the developing cortex.</p><h2>How we work</h2><p>We like ambitious questions, open methods, and projects where computation and experiments sharpen each other. The lab is being built as a supportive place for trainees to learn by doing, share unfinished ideas, and become thoughtful, independent scientists.</p></article>
